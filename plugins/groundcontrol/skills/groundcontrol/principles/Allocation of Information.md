 # Allocation of Information  
## Drawings  
  
> "The drawings are the graphic and pictorial portions of the contract documents showing the design, location and dimensions of the work, generally including plans, elevations, sections, details, schedules and diagrams."  
  
*American Institute of Architects (AIA) Document A201-2007 – General*  
  
  
## Schedules  
The *architectural drawing set* includes 'assembly of elements' schedules, e.g. door schedule and room (finishes) schedules.  
They are typically printed on the same size paper as the rest of the documentation set and include the SpA title block, drawing name and revision column.  
  
## Specifications  
> "The specifications are that portion of the contract document consisting of the written requirements for materials, equipment, systems, standards and workmanship for the work, and performance of the related services."  
  
*American Institute of Architects (AIA) Document A201-2007 – General Conditions of the Contract for Construction, Paragraph 1.1.6*  
  
## Content of Specification  
A typical architectural specification contains two categories of descriptions:  
*Technical*: e.g. materials, fabrication, workmanship, preparation, installation, tests, etc  
*Non-technical*: e.g. scope of work, delivery of materials, samples and shop drawings requirements, permits, guarantees, job conditions, cleaning, etc  
  
## Say It Once  
Provide a piece of information as few times as possible and only in the agreed place - ideally only once within the architectural drawing set, on a drawing or in a schedule.  
Do not repeat specifications in the drawing set.

## Notes

1. **Demolition** sits in the A02 series. No dedicated spA codex file exists for A02 — content requirements are covered by the Demolition component note and direct-instruction drafting convention.
2. **Lifts** — A00 legend and A40 content required only for bespoke lift car designs. Standard lift cars are selected from supplier's catalogue and approved via shop drawings; no design drawing required.
3. **Signage** — A00, A80 and Z entries apply only where wayfinding signage is within spA's contract scope. Statutory door signs (FIRE DOORS KEEP CLOSED etc.) are always scheduled in A70 regardless of contract scope.
4. **Furniture** — Z spec applies only if required by contract.
5. **Concrete** — the A40 entry indicates that stair and core drawings in A40 carry detail reference markers back to A50. The details themselves live in A50, not A40.
6. **Paint Finishes** — the A70 entry covers factory-applied finishes only (powder coat, anodising, factory paint). Site-applied paint to joinery frames, jambs and architraves is carried in A80/A60, not A70. The painter will not read A70.
7. **Floors** — three methods exist for documenting floor finishes: (1) room schedule in A80 (preferred); (2) 1:50 plans in A60 for complex spaces; (3) dedicated floor finishes plan subset within A10 or A60 where Option 1 is insufficient across the board.
